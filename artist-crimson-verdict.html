About Crimson Verdict

Crimson Verdict emerged from the underground hard rock scene with a mission: to create powerful, uncompromising music that speaks to those who've fought through hell and came out stronger. Fronted by a vocalist with whiskey-soaked raspy vocals reminiscent of Lzzy Hale, the band blends Halestorm's organic guitar foundation with In This Moment's theatrical intensity.

Their sound is a hybrid of accessible hard rock and dramatic metal, heavy enough for the mosh pit, melodic enough for arena sing-alongs. With crushing guitar riffs, thunderous drums, and vocals that can go from intimate whisper to full-power belt to brutal scream, Crimson Verdict refuses to be boxed into one genre. They're the soundtrack for warrior women who wear their scars as crowns.

Their debut album "Burn the Throne" (2026) is an empowerment anthem from start to finish, twelve tracks chronicling the destruction of expectations and the building of a new empire from the ashes. Their sophomore album "Beautiful Violence" takes a darker turn, diving into occult imagery, religious blasphemy, and toxic obsessive love with industrial metal intensity and brutal screams. From accessible hard rock to crushing heavy metal, this is music for those who refuse to bow.

Burn the Throne (2026)

Hard Rock / Theatrical Metal • 12 Tracks

Crimson Verdict's explosive debut album is a statement of defiance and empowerment. "Burn the Throne" chronicles the journey from destruction to creation, burning down the throne society built for you and building your own empire from the ashes. Every track is an anthem for those who've been through hell and refused to stay down.

From the opening manifesto "Burn the Throne" through the swagger of "War Paint" and the brutal intensity of "No Mercy," the album showcases the full range of Crimson Verdict's sound. Power ballads like "Iron Heart" and "Ashes & Crowns" prove they can be vulnerable without losing their edge, while high-energy rockers "Wolves & Wildfire" and "Unbreakable" are built for arena sing-alongs. The sultry danger of "Pretty Wreckage" and "Venom Kiss" shows their sensual side without sacrificing power.

The album closes with "New Empire", an epic, triumphant anthem looking forward to the future you built with your own hands. Produced with a hybrid approach that blends Halestorm's organic guitar-driven sound with In This Moment's theatrical intensity, "Burn the Throne" is accessible enough for rock radio but heavy enough for metalheads. Featuring raspy, whiskey-soaked vocals that can belt, scream, and whisper with equal power, massive gang vocal choruses, crushing guitar riffs, and lyrics that speak directly to survivors and fighters. This is the sound of rising from the ashes and claiming your power.

Beautiful Violence (2026)

Heavy Metal / Industrial Metal • 12 Tracks • Explicit

If "Burn the Throne" was about rising from destruction, "Beautiful Violence" is about embracing the chaos itself. This album doesn't apologize or explain. It's raw feminine rage wrapped in beauty, finding art in brutality, power in destruction. Crimson Verdict goes full theatrical darkness with industrial elements, crushing heaviness, and a vocal performance that ranges from haunting whispers to brutal screams.

Mixing three dark themes throughout the tracklist, occult witchcraft energy, religious blasphemy with twisted church imagery, and toxic obsessive romance, "Beautiful Violence" is significantly heavier than the debut. From the blasphemous church energy of opener "Blood Sermon" through the possessed intensity of "Witching Hour" and the dangerous seduction of "Pretty When I Scream," every track showcases the full range of Crimson Verdict's evolved sound. The album features Drop C and Drop B tuning for crushing heaviness, industrial and electronic elements woven throughout, orchestral moments mixed with devastating breakdowns, and slower doom-like crushing sections that hit like a freight train.

The album closes with "Kingdom of Ash", an epic, apocalyptic finale that brings all the themes together. Produced with In This Moment's "Blood" album energy meets Halestorm's most brutal moments, this is theatrical metal at its darkest. Vocals alternate between powerful clean singing and brutal harsh screams, often within the same track. This is music for embracing your inner monster, finding beauty in violence, and refusing to be anything less than terrifying and gorgeous at once. Not for the faint of heart.
