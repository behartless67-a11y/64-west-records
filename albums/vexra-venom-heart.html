About the Artist

Vexra emerged from the underground bass scene in Virginia, an anonymous producer and vocalist who refused to show her face. Performing in a custom LED mask and all black, she built a cult following on the strength of her music alone. No face. No name. Just Vexra. Her production sits at the collision point of emotional pop vocals and skull-rattling trap, bass house, and hardstyle. Think Krewella's raw vulnerability fused with Yellow Claw's aggression, filtered through a cyberpunk lens. Over five albums, Vexra evolved from anonymous masked performer to revealing her true identity as Elena Voss, integrating both sides of herself, and finally emerging as one complete woman who chose to keep the name that made her powerful.

About This Album

"Human" was the therapy. "Venom Heart" is what happens when a woman who spent four albums figuring out who she is finally KNOWS. She decides she's done apologizing for any of it.

Elena gave Vexra vulnerability. Vexra gave Elena teeth. Combined? A woman who will whisper something devastating in your ear and then blow the speakers out. She's not masked, not hiding, not having an identity crisis. She's whole, she's angry, she's sexual, she's free, and she has absolutely zero interest in making you comfortable.

This is the All White album from the color roadmap. It's not angelic white. It's clinical white. Padded room white. "I've been through therapy and now I'm more dangerous" white. White leather, white light, white heat.

The album is credited simply to Vexra. No slash, no Elena Voss. Because she's not two people anymore. She's one woman who contains everything, and that woman chose to keep the name that made her powerful.

This is Vexra's sexiest and most aggressive album simultaneously. The industrial heaviness of "Skin & Static" meets the emotional rawness of "Human," but now there's a confidence underneath it that neither album had. She's not screaming because she's hurt. She's screaming because she WANTS to. Dark pop meets industrial bass meets trip-hop. Think Billie Eilish's whispered menace + Grimes' chaos + HEALTH's industrial weight + FKA twigs' sexuality. Heavy sub bass, minimal but devastating beats, space between the hits.

The album closes with "Free (The Final Light)," the ultimate cathartic release. Freedom found not in masks or revelation, but in total acceptance of every contradictory piece of yourself. This is Vexra at her most dangerous. Not because she's hiding. Because she's not.
