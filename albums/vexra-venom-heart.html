About the Artist

VEXRA emerged from the underground bass scene in Virginia, an anonymous producer and vocalist who refused to show her face. Performing in a custom LED mask and all black, she built a cult following on the strength of her music alone. No face. No name. Just VEXRA. Her production sits at the collision point of emotional pop vocals and skull-rattling trap, bass house, and hardstyle. Think Krewella's raw vulnerability fused with Yellow Claw's aggression, filtered through a cyberpunk lens. Over four albums, VEXRA evolved from anonymous masked performer to revealing her true identity: Elena Voss, a woman from Virginia who built a persona to survive, then had to learn to live without it.

About This Album

After the introspective duality of "Human," VEXRA returns to the shadows with her most visceral work yet. "Venom Heart" is a full embrace of the darker side—no acoustic interludes, no stripped-down vulnerability. This is VEXRA unmasked but unleashed, channeling everything she learned about herself into ten tracks of aggressive, unapologetic electronic fury.

The production is harder, the vocals more processed and distorted, the bass deeper. Songs like "Appetite," "Throat," and "Pretty When I Bite" explore desire and hunger through a venomous lens, while "White Room" and "Drip" create hypnotic, claustrophobic soundscapes. "Venom IV (Solo)" serves as a dark centerpiece—a reference to the substance that gave her power, now internalized. The album closes with "Free (The Final Light)," a cathartic release that suggests freedom isn't found in revelation or masks, but in total acceptance of every contradictory piece of yourself.

This is VEXRA at her most dangerous—not because she's hiding, but because she's not.
