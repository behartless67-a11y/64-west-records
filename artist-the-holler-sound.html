About The Holler Sound

Brother Marcus grew up in Southwest Virginia coal country, discovering reggae at 19 and spending years in Jamaica studying roots music. When he returned home, he was determined to prove that mountain people and island people share the same soul. At Floyd Country Store, he met Lena Mae, a fourth-generation Appalachian musician with mountain soul in her blood. Together with the Dub Mountain Crew, they created The Holler Sound: where Blue Ridge meets Blue Mountains.

Their sound is authentic fusion, not novelty, not gimmick, but genuine cultural connection. Organic banjo plays reggae skank rhythm. Mountain fiddle replaces brass section. Appalachian harmonies float over deep dub bass. One-drop reggae drums anchor front porch melodies. Brother Marcus's vocals carry both mountain twang and reggae phrasing, while Lena Mae's fiddle and harmonies bring generations of Appalachian soul.

The Holler Sound celebrates what mountain people and island people share: hardworking spirit, survival through oppression, music as medicine, deep connection to the land, and love as the answer. Both cultures know struggle. Both know resilience. Both know that rhythm can heal. This is roots music meeting roots music, Jamaica and Appalachia finding common ground.

With banjo, fiddle, acoustic guitar, mandolin, dub bass, and one-drop drums, The Holler Sound creates mountain dub that honors both traditions. No electronics, no shortcuts, just organic instruments, conscious lyrics, and the proof that different mountains can speak the same language. Jah bless the holler. One love from the Ridge.

Roots & Ridges (2026)

Reggae Fusion / Appalachian Roots / Mountain Dub • 12 Tracks

The debut album proving that Blue Ridge and Blue Mountains speak the same language. "Roots & Ridges" is authentic fusion of Jamaican roots reggae and Appalachian mountain music, banjo playing reggae skank, fiddle replacing brass section, mountain harmonies over dub bass, one-drop rhythm anchoring front porch melodies.

Brother Marcus and Lena Mae lead the Dub Mountain Crew through twelve tracks exploring the cultural connection between mountain people and island people. From the opening statement "Rasta in the Holler" to the triumphant closer "Born in the Holler," every song celebrates what these cultures share: survival, resilience, music as medicine, and deep connection to the land.

Organic instruments only, banjo, fiddle, acoustic guitar, mandolin, dub bass, and steady one-drop drums. Dub effects (reverb, delay, echo) swim through acoustic textures. Brother Marcus's vocals carry mountain twang and reggae phrasing. Lena Mae's fiddle work and Appalachian harmonies bring four generations of mountain soul. No electronics, no shortcuts, just honest fusion.

Highlights include the spiritual anthem "Mountain Lion (Zion)," the atmospheric "Jah Ridge," the instrumental showcase "Banjo Skank," the party energy of "Hillbilly Riddim," and the emotional "Blue Mountain Brothers."
