# Elena Voss & Stella Hartley - "Tierra"
## COMPLETE ALBUM - 10 Tracks

**Artists:** Elena Voss & Stella Hartley
**Genre:** Flamenco-Pop / Indie Folk
**Label:** 64 West Records
**Year:** 2026
**Runtime:** ~38 minutes

**Concept:**
What lives below language. Elena's Latin roots fully unleashed — bilingual throughout, Spanish guitar as the lead instrument, flamenco percussion as the heartbeat. Stella anchors in English, her raspy voice the contrast that makes Elena's warmth land harder. The album is about the things that don't need translation: rhythm, roots, heritage, the 4AM hour, the current that pulls people together. Three tracks earn electronic shimmer on their biggest emotional moments (4, 6, 8). Everything else is organic.

**Relationship:** Creative partners, chosen family. Not romantic.

**Voice profiles:**
- **Elena Voss:** Smooth, warm, soprano, bilingual Spanish/English, grounded, folk-pop close mic — sings Spanish naturally, not performed
- **Stella Hartley:** Bright, raspy, higher-register, raw rock edge, English only — [raspy] on EVERY Stella line

**Sonic identity:**
- Spanish classical guitar as lead instrument throughout
- Palmas (handclaps) as rhythmic foundation
- Cajon / live percussion — organic not programmed
- Bass guitar warm and melodic
- Tracks 4, 6, 8: synth pads shimmer + supersaws warm added
- NO electronic elements on all other tracks

## PRODUCTION NOTES

**The album arc:**
- Tracks 1-3: Movement and energy (Palmas, Lluvia, Mercado) — establishing the Latin sound
- Tracks 4-5: Language and heat (Lengua, Brasa) — the intimacy of the bilingual world
- Track 6: The 4AM hour (Madrugada) — the stripped-down truth
- Tracks 7-8: Forces and heritage (Corriente, Herencia) — what carries people
- Track 9: Almost silence (Quietud) — the fullest nothing
- Track 10: The roots (Tierra) — the anthemic closer

**Electronic tracks (4, 6, 8):**
- Lengua: Shimmer on the language track — the electronic element represents the untranslatable space between words
- Madrugada: Atmospheric shimmer — 4AM has texture that pure organic doesn't capture
- Herencia: Warm supersaws — heritage deserves the biggest emotional drop on the album

**Track 9 (Quietud) is radical:**
The slowest, quietest, most stripped track in the entire Elena/Stella catalog. Just two voices and barely one guitar. No bass, no cajon, one or two palmas. Tests whether silence can be the most powerful thing on a record.

**Voice consistency:**
Every single Stella line tagged [raspy]. Elena sings more Spanish on this album than any previous Elena project — closer to 50/50 Spanish/English, with some tracks going deeper into Spanish in the verses.

**2-genre discipline:**
All prompts use flamenco pop + indie folk / indie electronic. 640-702 chars each.
