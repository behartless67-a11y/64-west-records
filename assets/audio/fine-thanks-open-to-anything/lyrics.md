# FINE THANKS - "Open to Anything (Within Reason)"
## COMPLETE ALBUM - 12 Tracks

**Artists:** Fine Thanks
**Genre:** British Indie Rock / Post-Punk
**Label:** 64 West Records
**Year:** 2026
**Runtime:** ~42 minutes

**Duo Members:**
- **Sam** (Voice 1) - FEMALE, lower/cynical Northern England (Manchester), deadpan, slow, devastating
- **Jess** (Voice 2) - FEMALE, higher/anxious Southern England, fast, spiraling, scandalized

**Album Concept:**
Two friends recounting the absolute warzone of modern dating - apps, dates, the bedroom, the aftermath. Sam delivers the wreckage in flat Manchester deadpan. Jess panics and harmonizes the worst details. The comedy engine: deliver the filth like a customer complaint. British understatement against escalating absurdity. Graphic, but the deadpan makes it funny instead of gross. Under it all: the friendship that survives every disaster.

**The dynamic (the dialed-in Fine Thanks formula):**
- Jess = anxious, fast, spiraling, scandalized, asks the horrified questions
- Sam = cynical, slow, deadpan, reports atrocities like reviewing a parcel
- Comedy lives in HYPER-SPECIFIC detail (exact numbers, exact quotes, exact timing)
- Choruses go big, both in unison, end on a flat devastating line
- Profanity is punctuation - British vernacular (wanker, prick, twat, knob, absolute bastard)
- Every track ends back at the group chat - the girls vs. the warzone

## ALBUM PRODUCTION NOTES

**The dialed-in Fine Thanks formula throughout:**
- Sam (Manchester, deadpan, slow) reports atrocities flat; Jess (Southern, anxious, fast) panics and asks the horrified questions
- Comedy lives in hyper-specific detail: exact numbers, exact quotes, exact timing ("ninety minutes," "two pumps, I counted," "we've met twice")
- The interruption is the comedic spine ("On your WHAT?!")
- Big unison choruses ending on flat devastating closers
- British vernacular profanity as punctuation (knob, crab, prick, twat, ming, absolute bastard)
- Every track ends back at the group chat / the friendship - the girls vs. the warzone

**Emotional arc:**
- Tracks 1-9: Escalating dating horror, played for deadpan laughs
- Track 10 ("Left on Read"): The pivot - drops the armour, the quiet real sting underneath. Lets the album breathe and shows the vulnerability the jokes protect.
- Track 11 ("Teeth"): Back to full comedy, the most unhinged track - relief after the soft moment
- Track 12 ("Actually Quite Nice"): The closer - someone's finally normal and she's suspicious of it, then reluctantly lets her guard down. Earns a warm ending after the warzone. The bar was in hell; someone stepped through.

**Genre discipline:** Every prompt uses exactly 2 genres (indie rock + post-punk / garage punk / dream pop / jangle pop / dirty pop). All prompts 650-720 chars, well under 900.

**Vocal tags:** [Verse 1 - Sam, deadpan] / [Jess, anxious/fast] format throughout, consistent with the Fine Thanks catalog.

**Keep the explicit deadpan, not raunchy:** The filth is recounted like a customer complaint. Flat delivery of outrageous content. That contrast is the whole brand.
